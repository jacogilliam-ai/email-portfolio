The brief I gave myself

Mom 'n 'em announced a latte art throwdown on Facebook with a Fellow Series 1 espresso machine as the grand prize, a $15 competitor entry, a raffle, smashburgers and a DJ.

Everything in the email comes from that post. Nobody asked for this.

One email is an announcement. Three is a campaign.

Mon 15th
Latte art throwdown, Friday the 25th
Announce. Full detail, calendar add.
Tue 22nd
Three days out: burgers, wine, and a bracket
Remind. Leads with the food, not the coffee.
Fri 25th
Tonight at 5:30 in Madisonville
Show up. Two sentences and directions.

The second email leads with smashburgers and wine because the list is mostly not baristas. A bracket is the draw for a tenth of them. Giving up a Friday night needs a different reason.

Decisions worth defending

The prize leads, not the competition. A $1,500 machine is the hook for everyone who does not care about latte art, which is most of the list. The bracket is the draw for maybe a tenth of them.

Date and address are live text, not artwork, in the announce. Gmail blocks images by default for unknown senders, Outlook.com inverts backgrounds in dark mode, and a screen reader gets an alt tag. The two facts a reader actually needs survive all three.

Competing and the raffle are quiet. Small audiences, paid actions. Making them loud would invert the hierarchy against the free primary CTA, which is what the other 95% of the list is deciding about.

Every send has a path for people who cannot come. Most of a coffee shop’s list will not give up a Friday. Without a shop link they reach the bottom with nothing to do, and a dead send is a wasted one.

The raffle is the strongest mechanic in the whole event. “You have to be there to win” makes a $10 ticket a firmer commitment to attend than any calendar add. That is why it gets its own block rather than a line.

Every primary CTA lands above the mobile fold, which sits near 740px on an iPhone after mail-app chrome. Email 2 originally put its calendar button at 1,142px because the artwork sat between the copy and the ask. Moving the artwork below the CTA brought it to 738.

Length falls as urgency rises. 3,569 to 1,698 to 1,277. By the third email a reader can tell at a glance that this one is quick, which is exactly what you want at 3pm on a Friday.

Every image is under 400KB and each asset URL carries a content hash, so a replaced file can never serve stale from a CDN or browser cache.
