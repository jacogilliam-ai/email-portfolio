Building the list

Aurean launched from zero. No existing audience, no ad spend, no influencer seeding. The list was built entirely on organic writing: posts in Reddit and Facebook communities that earned 200,000+ views, including a single post that reached 120,000.

Those readers landed on a password-protected Shopify storefront and entered an email to be notified. The list peaked at 375 subscribers before launch.

Small list, and worth saying plainly. It behaved like one: engagement on a hand-built organic list runs well above what a large paid-acquisition list produces, and the rates below should be read in that light.

The Gen 5 Survey inverted it: the lowest open rate of any send and by far the highest click rate, because the people who opened it had something specific to do.

Two things in that flow are worth pointing at. The order check repeats before every send rather than once at the top, so somebody who buys between email one and email two stops hearing about their cart immediately. And the final branch splits on list membership: a founding waitlist member who abandons has a different reason than a stranger who abandons, so they were never going to get the same message.

What happened

Aurean wound down in August 2026. The product didn't work on unit economics: manufacturing cost and the price it had to carry never reconciled, and no amount of email was going to fix that.

The program itself did what it was built to do. A list from nothing, mid-forties open rates sustained across seventeen sends, a clean sending reputation, and a flow architecture I'd build the same way again. The lesson I took was about sequencing rather than copy: I built the full lifecycle before I had validated that anyone would pay the price the product required.
